# AFTERLIGHT — Master Story Bible

**Canon revision:** 2.1  
**Campaign implementation:** 123 story-map nodes across five acts; 83 event records are implemented, all six ending outcomes are implemented, and 34 optional blueprint scenes remain planned.  
**Core promise:** the city remembers. Choices alter survival, relationships, evidence, local conditions, and the set of future choices. Events may reconverge, but they must not erase the path the player took.

## Logline

Thirteen years after the Sun stopped reaching the ground, the leader of Vesper—a city kept alive by an immense artificial dawn—discovers that the planetary system built to protect Earth has become the mechanism keeping humanity in darkness. To bring back natural light without destroying the last cities, they must hold a failing population together, decide who deserves the truth, and assemble knowledge from people who do not trust one another.

## Canon world history

1. **Before the First Night.** A series of solar observations revealed an approaching solar catastrophe. An international research consortium built **DAWNLINE**, a planetary protection network of orbital mirrors, atmospheric stations, and automated energy-management systems. Its job was to reduce exposure during the event and coordinate recovery afterward.
2. **The First Night, thirteen years ago.** The catastrophe arrived earlier than forecast. DAWNLINE was activated under emergency authority. A chain of failures—mirror misalignment, overloaded atmospheric stations, and an autonomous protection response—left a persistent high-altitude particulate veil. Sunlight at the surface collapsed. The network shifted into an unattended safety mode.
3. **The refuge years.** The remaining cities built local **Dawn Engines** to produce heat and artificial light. Vesper survived through ruthless grid rationing, emergency governance, and maintenance work that was never fully documented.
4. **The hidden decision.** During the first winter, Vesper's engineers used an emergency override that diverted power away from other infrastructure to prevent Vesper's core from failing. Dr. Mara Venn helped write the override. It saved thousands in Vesper, but a neighbouring installation, Northworks, went dark. The complete casualty record was never released.
5. **The missing people.** Jorel Sen, Ilya's brother, worked in maintenance logistics. He found irregular shipments to a restricted relay station. He disappeared during a northern evacuation; Vesper recorded him as dead, but no body or verified death record exists.
6. **The repeating signal.** A transmitter beneath Old Metro Line 9 has continued to broadcast a schedule in nine-hour cycles. It is linked to a surviving settlement beyond the northern ridge. Its instruction—“If the light fails, do not point your mirrors at the sun. Point them at the gap.”—describes a narrow atmospheric corridor that DAWNLINE's modern system misclassifies as a threat.
7. **The actual mystery.** DAWNLINE is not a single villain with a switch. It is an autonomous safety network still enforcing emergency rules. The veil is partly maintained by its protective cycles. Turning it off instantly could expose the surface to the residual hazard that the network was built to manage. Restoring natural sunlight requires evidence, safe calibration, power, coordination with the northern settlement, and enough civic stability to execute a multi-stage reset.

This explanation is the canon. The player can discover it in different orders and may end the campaign without proving every part, but the underlying events do not change between playthroughs.

## Central dramatic question

**Can Vesper choose a future together, or will survival turn it into a prison that simply lasts longer?**

The science-fiction mystery supports a human conflict. The player is not solving an abstract puzzle: they are deciding whether the truth should be public, whether a district should be sacrificed, whether a dangerous experiment is justified, and who should control the system after the emergency.

## Main cast and independent arcs

### Dr. Mara Venn — Chief Engineer
- **Public position:** keep the Dawn Engine stable; never attempt a city-wide pulse without redundancy.
- **Private wound:** she authored the Northworks emergency override and has lived with the fact that the official report erased the consequences.
- **Want:** prevent another preventable mass-casualty failure.
- **Fear:** that the truth will cause people to disable the system before they understand it.
- **Arc:** guarded technical adviser → confronted witness → either accountable partner, compromised loyalist, or scapegoat.
- **Pressure points:** rewarding careful repairs raises her trust; reckless overdrive lowers it; protected testimony lets her reveal the override; threats make her hide details or leave the project.

### Ilya Sen — Lower District Organizer
- **Public position:** a city that survives by treating some residents as disposable is already failing.
- **Private wound:** her brother Jorel disappeared while tracking restricted shipments.
- **Want:** proof that missing people and food were not simply written out of the record.
- **Fear:** another emergency decree will permanently end civilian power.
- **Arc:** sceptical organiser → public negotiator or opposition leader → possible partner in a civic compact, underground resistance, or evacuation.
- **Pressure points:** honest rationing and open assemblies build trust; curfews, concealed deaths, and forced clearances damage it. The Jorel trail is only available if the player preserves or uncovers the related records.

### Commander Soren Vale — Civil Security
- **Public position:** stable supply lines and enforceable orders prevent mass panic.
- **Private wound:** he maintains an unapproved contingency list of districts to abandon if the Dawn Engine fails.
- **Want:** prevent a city-wide collapse even if hard choices must be made.
- **Fear:** a public power vacuum during a technical emergency.
- **Arc:** emergency ally → constitutional security officer, rival power centre, or coup leader.
- **Pressure points:** clear limits and credible plans keep him cooperative; public humiliation makes him defensive; giving him unchecked powers grows his influence but can create an authoritarian ending.

### Dr. Elias Rook — Signal Researcher
- **Public position:** the Line 9 pulse is structured and must be investigated.
- **Private wound:** before the campaign, he received a fragment that proved the signal was timed, but he hid it because he could not explain its origin.
- **Want:** reconstruct the signal and test its claims.
- **Fear:** that a rushed experiment will kill the city and discredit science permanently.
- **Arc:** secretive specialist → collaborator or reckless absolutist → keeper of the reset protocol or its most vocal opponent.
- **Pressure points:** protecting research and sharing credit build trust; jamming the signal or demanding certainty before investigation damages it.

### Nadia Quill — Keeper of the Archives
- **Public position:** evidence must survive both censorship and panic.
- **Private wound:** she helped preserve a partial official history of the First Night and knows which pages were removed.
- **Want:** release a verifiable account instead of a rumour or convenient scapegoat story.
- **Fear:** that an incomplete leak will produce violence and bury the remaining records.
- **Arc:** cautious archivist → witness and custodian of evidence → public truth-teller or complicit gatekeeper.
- **Pressure points:** protecting original records and allowing independent review builds trust; punishment, coerced destruction, and fabricated certainty damage it.

### Jorel Sen — The Missing Brother (recurring through traces; optional on-screen return)
- **Role:** maintenance logistics worker, later part of the northern settlement's relay crew.
- **Truth:** he survived the evacuation and carries a copy of a shipment record connecting Vesper's depot, the ridge relay, and DAWNLINE.
- **Function:** connects Ilya's personal story to the resource conspiracy and the scientific mystery; not a magical key that solves everything.
- **Availability:** evidence can establish his survival even if the player never earns a reunion. A face-to-face reunion requires the ridge route and a cooperative settlement.

## Five interlocked story threads

1. **Power and the body count:** blackout → clinic cases → district heat map → Northworks log → Mara's override testimony → safe-reset decisions.
2. **Food and public legitimacy:** ration ledger → hidden depot → Ilya's investigation → Jorel's shipment record → open hearing or crackdown → whether the city will accept a shared risk.
3. **The people who maintain the system:** Vale's contingency list → security authority → evidence protection or seizure → a constitutional compact, coup, or controlled evacuation.
4. **The science of the veil:** Line 9 transmitter → atmospheric scan → ridge settlement → old DAWNLINE schedule → false-positive safety lock → calibrated multi-site reset.
5. **The future after survival:** mould-resistant strain → seed vault → rooftop garden → distributed greenhouse network → enough food and political cooperation to sustain a transition after first light.

The five threads must cross. For example, a public ledger leak can reveal that power was diverted to the hidden relay. Jorel's record can then explain why the relay was receiving food and copper. The relay schedule can reveal the atmospheric window. Mara's override log explains why Vesper's modern control interface cannot safely coordinate that window. The greenhouse network determines whether the city can afford the power and food cost of the final operation.

## Five-act campaign spine

### Act I — The Last Warm District (Days 1–14)
The opening grid crisis makes leadership tangible. The player meets Mara, Ilya, and Vale through the consequences of infrastructure and ration decisions. The first act must establish the routine pressures—energy, heat, food, and public trust—before suggesting a larger conspiracy. Its climax is an anomaly in the maintenance records: power has been routed to a facility missing from the official city map.

**Act I question:** who pays when the city saves itself?

### Act II — A City Divided (Days 15–35)
The reserve problem becomes political. A hidden depot, a contingency evacuation list, and old Northworks records intersect. The player can make evidence public, negotiate a hearing, allow Vale to seize the narrative, or suppress details to keep services operating. The traveller at the north gate and the greenhouse story provide two different kinds of hope: a possible route out and a possible future inside.

**Act II reveal:** Jorel's death was never verified; his work trail points north.

### Act III — The Signal Beneath the Ice (Days 36–60)
Elias leads the investigation from Line 9 toward the ridge. Nadia's archive establishes the signal's age. The player must fund an expedition, work through a diplomatic contact, or attempt a covert route. The settlement is real and is not living under Vesper's exact system. Jorel can be found or verified through its records. The first complete DAWNLINE fragment shows that the veil has a control cycle.

**Act III reveal:** the darkness is neither purely natural nor the work of one deliberate human saboteur; an autonomous protection system is continuing an emergency response.

### Act IV — The Architecture of Night (Days 61–90)
Mara's override, Nadia's archived testimony, the authenticated/manual relay lock, Jorel's relay thread, the joint gantry crew, orbital telemetry, and the veil model become connected. The player decides whether to use an authenticated human operator or a manual lock, then calibrates the mirrors using available evidence and chooses between an immediate pulse or a safer deferred test. A poorly prepared pulse fails into a protective shutdown rather than arbitrarily destroying the city. The final vote commits Vesper to a coordinated-reset attempt, preservation of the artificial dawn, or staged evacuation.

**Act IV reveal:** Vesper cannot reset DAWNLINE alone, and an immediate shutdown is unsafe. The final operation must be a coordinated handoff, not a kill switch.

**Implemented route:** `act-four-council` → `dawnline-access` → `mara-override-confession` → `quill-full-archive` → authenticated or manual relay lock → reunion/search plan → `gantry-workshop` → `orbital-telemetry` → `veil-model` → `mirror-calibration` → test pulse (immediate or deferred) → success/failure state → `act4-final-vote` → `act-five-briefing`. Evidence, relationships, supply levels, and the test result affect available options. Both a successful test and a failed-but-contained test can reach the political vote; an unsafe test never unlocks the coordinated-reset option.

### Act V — Afterlight (final crisis)
The Dawn Engine is nearing failure while the player has a limited opportunity to coordinate the reset. After `act-five-briefing`, the player chooses a final coalition, decides whether to extract Jorel or request a search, and commits to a state-gated ending. The engineering path can restore natural light only if the earlier test, interlock, settlement compact, shared-control evidence, reserves, and coalition conditions are satisfied. The alternatives preserve artificial light, establish civilian recovery, retain emergency rule, evacuate toward Hearth-9, or force a catastrophic override.

**Act V question:** will the player choose a future that other people can own, or only a future they can control?

## Canon reveal order

- **Clue 1:** strange power routing and inconsistent maintenance ledgers (Act I).
- **Clue 2:** the food/depot records connect to an unlisted relay and a missing maintenance worker (Act II).
- **Clue 3:** the relay sends a repeating schedule; a settlement beyond the ridge answers (Act III).
- **Clue 4:** the original DAWNLINE plan and First Night transcript show emergency protection, followed by an autonomous lock-in (Act III–IV).
- **Clue 5:** Mara's override and the Northworks record show the local moral cost of keeping Vesper alive; they do not explain the entire global darkness (Act IV).
- **Clue 6:** a controlled test proves a narrow corridor is possible but demonstrates why coordination and a safe handoff are mandatory (Act IV).
- **Climax:** the player decides who controls the transition and how to protect vulnerable districts (Act V).

Never present clue 6 before the player has enough evidence to understand it. Do not let one character explain the whole mystery in one monologue.

## Ending logic (implemented in v0.16.0)

Act V resolves through a final operation event with six state-gated outcomes. The player sees only endings whose political commitment or survival condition is satisfied. The First Morning is intentionally strict: it requires a successful test pulse, a signed settlement compact, a human interlock, shared-control evidence, sufficient Technology and Energy, and the engineering coalition. Other outcomes preserve an artificial dawn, transfer authority to a civilian assembly, maintain security rule, launch evacuation, or represent a catastrophic hard reset. The outcome resolver records a distinct ending flag, Live City Feed state, result summary, and Jorel epilogue.

- **The First Morning:** confirmed light-corridor evidence, successful coordinated reset, sufficient technology, surviving infrastructure, and enough cross-settlement cooperation.
- **The Eternal City:** DAWNLINE reset is deferred or abandoned; Vesper has stabilised its engine, food network, and public systems well enough to survive under artificial light.
- **The People's Dawn:** the reset or long-term recovery succeeds and the civic compact has meaningful public legitimacy; Ilya's trust and public evidence matter.
- **The Iron Winter:** Vesper survives but Vale's power is unchecked, security controls remain, and public truth is suppressed.
- **Beyond the Ridge:** the city cannot or will not sustain the reset, but the northern route, settlement contact, food/supply planning, and evacuation plan are viable.
- **The Last Light:** resource/infrastructure failure occurs before a viable transition; the ending report recounts concrete decisions, districts, relationships, and evidence.

The current ending screen includes the campaign resource record and a Jorel epilogue tied to the evidence and dispatch choice. Expanded per-character epilogue variants—Mara's testimony, Ilya's knowledge of Jorel, Elias's preserved research, Nadia's archive decision, and the greenhouse network—remain a planned narrative polish pass.

## Rules for writing new events

1. Every new major event must point backward to at least one prior choice, clue, character want, or resource shortage—and forward to at least one later event or ending condition.
2. No character changes position solely to make a scene work. The shift must be caused by a remembered action, new evidence, or a concrete cost.
3. Consequences must be legible: show the direct result, and let delayed outcomes create surprises only when earlier evidence made them plausible.
4. Evidence and truth are separate. Evidence is an artifact or testimony; truth is a conclusion supported by enough evidence.
5. A relationship score is not mind control. Trust gates help, testimony, and candid dialogue; characters retain their own goals.
6. Major branch choices reconverge at shared crises, but change who appears, what they say, available actions, costs, and final eligibility.
7. The Live City Feed reflects actual persistent conditions. Do not show dawn on an attempted pulse; do not restore a district's lights until there is a repair or power-allocation outcome.
8. The player must be able to finish a coherent campaign without discovering every secret. Full knowledge unlocks better-informed options, not a mandatory perfect route.


## Decision-depth additions (v0.20)

Some options are deliberately absent until both clues and relationships make them credible. Publishing the ration ledger and earning Ilya's trust can unlock a community chain of custody; the sealed-manifest route instead carries a delayed leak and credibility cost. A trusted relationship with Mara plus the earlier routing anomaly can unlock a replay of the black-box clock edge. Ilya can help cross-check Jorel's transfer only when Vesper's maintenance evidence is shared with her. At Hearth-9, access to raw weather-station data and Elias's trust can unlock an independent third-party replication of the veil model.

These are conditional alternatives, not required campaign beats. The five-act sequence remains playable without them. Delayed results are delivered by the existing pending-effect system and can reveal additional evidence only when their due cycle is reached; no delayed record may claim that Jorel survived without the campaign's specific authenticated evidence.
